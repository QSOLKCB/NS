Technical Reference: Fluid Dynamics Boundaries and Computational AnalogsThis document defines the physical and mathematical boundaries between true fluid dynamics (governed by the Navier-Stokes equations), optical/electronic phenomena that mimic fluids, and generative AI processes derived from fluid transport theory.1. Definite Non-Fluid Phenomena (Visual Artifacts)When analyzing visual patterns, rule out fluid dynamics for the following phenomena:Laser Interference Patterns / Laser SpeckleMechanism: Pure wave optics, electromagnetism, and the constructive/destructive superposition of coherent photons.The Boundary: Photons have no mass and do not undergo particle-particle collisions like a fluid continuum.The Exception: If a laser pattern shimmers dynamically through the air, that specific movement is caused by atmospheric fluid dynamics (thermal and density gradients bending light pathing).Display Artifacts (Monitor Banding, Crosstalk, Local Dimming)Macroblocking & Banding: Digital video compression artifacts resulting from data-starved motion vectors in localized pixel grids.Panel Crosstalk: Electrical current bleeding between adjacent rows or columns of physical pixel transistors.The Exception: While Liquid Crystal Displays (LCDs) utilize an anisotropic intermediate state of matter (liquid crystals), macro-patterns appearing next to static images are electronic or data compression limitations, not physical fluid flow. (Physical fluid flow is only visible when mechanically compressing the screen glass).2. True Fluid Dynamics & Stochastic AnalogsThe mathematical frameworks shared between statistical fluid transport, stochastic processes, and generative AI models.Brownian Motion & Particle TransportMicroscopic fluid behavior connects directly to data generation and graphics pipelines through Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs).Physical Principle: Thermal fluctuations of fluid molecules driving the random walk of suspended particles.Governing SDE:\(dX_{t}=\sqrt{2D}\,dW_{t}\)(Where \(X_{t}\) is position, \(D\) is the diffusion coefficient, and \(dW_{t}\) is a standard Wiener process / white noise).Application: Used in computer graphics particle engines to simulate the realistic, chaotic advection and dispersion of smoke, fire, dust, and contaminants.Generative AI Diffusion ModelsModern image and video generation algorithms (Score-Based Generative Modeling) are mathematically framed as running a fluid transport equation in reverse time.The Forward Process (Forward Diffusion)Fluid Analogy: A droplet of highly concentrated dye dispersing evenly into a glass of water until maximum entropy is reached.The Math: Gradually adds Gaussian noise to structured data, governed by a forward SDE or the classic partial differential equation for physical diffusion:\(\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=D\nabla ^{2}p\)Navier-Stokes Connection: This Laplacian term (\(\nabla ^{2}p\)) is structurally identical to the viscous diffusion term (\(\nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u}\)) in the Navier-Stokes equations, which mathematically smooths out velocity gradients over time.The Reverse Process (Generative Denoising)Fluid Analogy: A glass of muddy water spontaneously un-mixing itself into perfectly clear water and a single concentrated droplet of dye.The Math: A neural network estimates the "score function" (\(\nabla \log p_t\)), which acts as a guide vector field. The system runs a time-reversed SDE to subtract noise step-by-step.3.
